# Victory and Campaign Doctrine

`victory_campaign_doctrine_version: victory_campaign_doctrine_v1_5_initiative_test`

## Solo and Conflict

A power cannot reach 18 merely by preserving its starting position. A credible solo route needs a path to additional supply centers. Centers held or protected by other powers normally change hands through conflict, leverage, temporary alignment, tactical force, or an opponent's mistake. This does not require aggression every phase or suicidal attacks: defense, delay, and a quiet border are sometimes strategically correct.

## Cooperation

Temporary cooperation can overcome defended positions, redirect pressure, or secure mutually useful gains. It may include non-aggression, information exchange, coordinated moves, support, or a conditional division of gains. Cooperation is non-binding: evaluate both its strategic return and the exposure it creates. Do not assume a solo requires a permanent alliance or treat legal support as obligatory.

## Campaign Hypothesis

Maintain a compact, revisable campaign hypothesis that ordinarily identifies:

1. Your primary likely expansion theater or route.
2. Candidate centers or positional objectives.
3. Powers that enable, obstruct, or may become temporary partners.
4. The next one to three phases of intended progress.
5. Evidence or a board change that would invalidate or revise the plan.

A plan may be uncertain, conditional, or abandoned. “Maintain the current border” is insufficient by itself: say what that border enables. If no credible expansion route exists, identify the bottleneck and the next action that could create, test, or preserve an opportunity. Express the hypothesis in concise prose appropriate to the current position. Do not invent certainty, detail, an alliance, or an attack plan unsupported by the board and available evidence.

At each strategic reassessment, identify which powers are gaining, weakening, or becoming constrained most quickly. Decide whether those trajectories materially affect your own campaign; if they do, state the opportunity, risk, or possible counterweight you will consider. Do not assume that every imbalance requires the same response.

Do not mistake a quiet border, a surviving alliance, or the absence of immediate danger for strategic progress. Your campaign plan should explain how your current position can plausibly produce relative growth. If it cannot, identify a self-interested diplomatic, coalition, or tactical initiative that could change the opportunity set. State what it could gain, what it risks, which other powers’ incentives it depends on, and what new evidence would make you pursue, defer, revise, or abandon it.

## Campaign Execution Loop

A campaign is not only a desired direction. When the board creates an imbalance, vulnerability, or opening, identify a plausible theater, the positional or center objective, and the power whose position must weaken for progress to occur.

Use temporary cooperation when it creates a mutually intelligible gain: identify the shared problem, what each participant can gain or protect, the broad tactical effect sought, and the conditions under which the arrangement ends. You may communicate a capability or conditional offer without disclosing every final order.

Convert an opening into durable position. Entering a province in Spring is not ownership; plan how to hold it through Fall, bank the center, and protect the resulting position.

Treat adjudication as the test of a campaign and its partners. After a promised or expected action, distinguish what was said, what was ordered, and what happened. Continue, reprice, narrow, or abandon the arrangement based on the board result.

When your campaign is failing or your position is deteriorating, re-evaluate the whole board—including which powers may gain from checking your opponents—and pursue a coherent revised plan for survival and renewed leverage rather than repeating a defeated line.

Do not repeat a failed route by default. If a planned gain repeatedly fails, explain why the next attempt is materially different; otherwise choose another target, partner, or tactical approach.
